    How to run it

    Easy means easy

    • You can talk in full sentences. If you can't, slow down or walk. Walking is part of the plan.
    • At your size, easy will probably be around 7:30 to 9:00 per km at first. That's normal, and it's where the fitness comes from.
    • The only quick bits are strides: 20 seconds of fast, relaxed running, then walk back until you've recovered. Not sprints.

    Run / walk

    • From week 9, long runs use the race pattern: run 4 minutes, walk 1 minute, from the first minute. It keeps your legs fresh for longer and makes the 6-hour limit comfortable.
    • If the baseline run in week 0 was hard, do every run in weeks 1 to 4 as run/walk: 1 min run and 1 min walk in week 1, then 2/1, 3/1 and 4/1. Same total minutes.

    Strength, twice a week

    About 20 minutes after the Tuesday and Thursday runs. No gym needed.

    • Calf raises 3 × 15, one leg at a time once it's easy. The most important one.
    • Squats 3 × 12
    • Reverse lunges 2 × 10 each leg
    • Glute bridges 3 × 15, then single-leg
    • Plank 3 × 30–45 s
    • Side plank 2 × 30 s each side
    • Stand on one leg 2 × 30 s each, eyes closed once it's easy

    Food and weight

    • Losing weight makes every run easier on your legs. Let it happen slowly, about 0.25 to 0.5 kg a week. That could be 6 to 10 kg by April without hurting your training.
    • Don't crash-diet. A big calorie cut while your distances go up is how beginners get injured, sick or stuck.
    • Eat normally on long-run days, with carbs before and after. Make the cut on rest days.
    • Protein with every meal. About 1.6 g per kg of body weight a day helps you keep muscle while you lose fat.
    • On runs over about 90 minutes, eat during the run (gels, dates, jelly sweets), even while losing weight.
    • Weigh yourself once a week on the same morning and log it in that week's card. Ignore daily jumps; that's mostly water.

    Winter in Cluj

    • December to February means dark by about 16:30, ice and below-zero mornings.
    • Dress in layers. You should feel a little cold for the first 10 minutes.
    • After dark, wear a headlamp or a reflective vest.
    • Icy? Do weekday runs on a treadmill at 1% incline. Don't do long runs on ice; move them a day or split them on a treadmill.

    Protect the bet

    1. Pain that makes you limp or gets worse as you run: stop and walk home.
    2. A spot on your shin or foot that hurts to press and lasts more than 2 or 3 days: stop running and see a physio. Stress injuries are how beginners lose months.
    3. Missed a few days? Carry on. Missed a week? Repeat the week before. Missed two or more? Go back two weeks.
    4. A cold in your head only: an easy run is fine. Fever or chest: no running.
    5. Don't speed up the plan because you feel good. Heart and lungs improve faster than tendons and bones.
    6. Sleep 7 hours or more. It does more than any gadget.

    Race day

    • Target about 7:40 per km including walks: halfway in about 2:42, finish in about 5:25. That leaves roughly 18 minutes of buffer on the halfway cutoff.
    • Start near the back and run 4 / walk 1 from the very first minute. Don't go faster than 7:15 per km in the first 10 km, however good it feels.
    • There are 7 aid stations with water and isotonic drink, plus fruit at km 11, 16 and 18.8. Walk through every one and drink.
    • A gel at 45 minutes, then one every 40 minutes. Use the ones you trained with.
    • Nothing new on race day: same shoes, kit, breakfast and gels as your 30 km rehearsal in week 24.
    • The second lap is the real race. If 4 / 1 gets hard after 30 km, switch to 3 / 1. You'll still be well inside 6 hours.
    • Keep the finisher diploma. It's your proof for the bet.

    Plan B

    • The bet needs a finished marathon by 31 December 2027, so April isn't your only chance.
    • If the January checkpoint goes badly or you get injured, switch to the half marathon in April (allowed until 31 January) and run a full marathon in the autumn.
    • The Bucharest Marathon is usually the second weekend of October. The 2027 date isn't announced yet.
    • No medical certificate is needed for Cluj; you sign that you're healthy. A quick GP check (blood pressure, ECG) before the long runs start is still worth it.
